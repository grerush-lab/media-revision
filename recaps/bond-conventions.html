    Product context

    • Released 19 December 1974, starring Roger Moore as 007, only his second appearance as Bond.
    • Based on a book by Ian Fleming. Produced by British company Eon (Everything or Nothing) Productions and distributed by United Artists.
    • Budget of about $7 million; it grossed over $97 million worldwide.
    • To reflect the popularity of martial arts films (stars like Bruce Lee and Jackie Chan), it had Kung Fu scenes and was shot mainly in Asia: Hong Kong, Thailand and Macau.
    • The poster artwork was painted by illustrator Robert McGinnis.

    Historical context

    • Before the 1990s, illustrations were much more common on film posters because of the limited technology available.
    • The film was set in the middle of the 1973 energy crisis, when oil-producing Arab nations declared an oil embargo.
    • The crisis had short- and long-term effects on politics and the economy across the globe.
    • This is hinted at through the poster's iconography of the power plant in the lower left corner and the energy beam aimed at Bond.

    Exam tip: always link a context to something you can point at on the poster.

    How does the trailer use Bond conventions differently from the poster?

    • The poster is static: it packs many conventions into one montage and leaves the audience to piece the narrative together. The trailer shows them in sequence, with editing that builds pace.
    • The trailer adds sound: music, voice-over and dialogue let it use conventions a poster can't, such as the one-liners and the theme.
    • Both use enigma codes: they reveal the threat (a gun with Bond's name on a bullet) without revealing how the story ends.
    • Both rely on the audience's prior knowledge of the franchise. The conventions work as intertextual shorthand for "a Bond film".

How this activity works ▾

One piece of evidence appears at a time. Drag it (or tap a category button) into the Media Language category it belongs to. Look closely at the cover on the left before you decide.

You get two attempts per card. Get it right and you'll see a short explanation — read it, then click "Next" when you're ready. Get it wrong once and a "Try again" button appears so you can have another go. Get it wrong twice and the correct answer is revealed (that card gets flagged for review in your results); click "Next" to continue once you've read it.

Some evidence fits two categories. When that happens, after you place it, it will come back round once more so you can also file it under its second category.

Genre vs Narrative — they're not the same thing: Genre is about conventions of the form (what all men's lifestyle magazine covers tend to do). Narrative is about the specific story being told about this person, in this issue.
Why some evidence fits two boxes: the giant golden gun is a visual choice (Images) and it's the literal object the film's plot revolves around (Narrative). Spotting overlap like this earns top marks in an exam answer.
